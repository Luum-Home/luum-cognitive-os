## Decision
This ADR deliberately commits to nothing except naming the decision space. It documents two hypothetical shapes — Shape A (current solo-swarm: one maintainer, multiple IDEs/sessions/agents, local file locks, no version-pinning, no formal ADR review quorum) and Shape B (hypothetical multi-maintainer: scoped Engram permissions with federation, a real lock coordinator like Redis/Valkey, versioned skill registry, ADR review with quorum and code-owners) — without building Shape B. It also codifies five "provisional constraints" to keep Shape A from painting itself into a corner (no hardcoded maintainer filesystem paths, harness-portability frontmatter on every hook, evidence-blocks on `core`/`team` tier claims, `topic_key`-namespaced Engram storage, and transferability metadata on silent-failure allowlist entries).

## Why
Nothing about the system's current health requires a fork — the 2026-05-03 boring-reliability audit confirmed 8/10 control-plane tools REAL and the codebase is operationally sound under solo-maintainer assumptions. But specific design decisions already make the single-maintainer assumption explicit and load-bearing: ADR-131 names the maintainer's machine as the accepted SPOF; the `maintainer` adoption tier is literally defined as "solo-swarm mode"; and a second maintainer cannot inherit the maintainer's personally-audited mental cache of 1,580 accepted silent failures. The ADR exists so future-self (or a future maintainer) doesn't have to re-derive this question from scratch when a real trigger fires.

## Consequences
Of writing this ADR: names the decision space and makes the five provisional constraints explicit enough to influence day-to-day choices (new hook frontmatter, new ADR tier claims) — but risks creating pressure to act prematurely, which the `exploration` status is meant to resist. Of NOT forking when a trigger eventually fires: the single-maintainer assumption hardens further and some Shape B changes (especially Engram federation) become progressively more expensive to build later. Of forking too early: doubles the maintenance surface immediately and builds infrastructure for users who don't yet exist — the classic speculative-generality failure.

## Status & current state
Status is `exploration` — explicitly not committing to any architectural change. Two audit scripts now make the boundary measurable rather than philosophical: `scripts/cos-tier-claim-audit` requires ADRs claiming `tier: core`/`team` to carry a `cos-boring-reliability` evidence block, and `scripts/cos-manifest-tier-claim-audit` audits the primitive-lifecycle manifest for undertransferable `core`/`team` claims and `maintainer`-cache candidates for demotion. As of 2026-05-03 the silent-failure allowlist is fully owned/dated but still contains `maintainer_cache`-state entries that emit a `shape-b-transferability-debt` warning rather than a hard failure. Recommendation stands: stay in Shape A; four candidate triggers (weakest to strongest: interest expressed → PR lands → maintainer wants a break → external consumer demand) are named, and only trigger 3 or 4 should move this ADR from `exploration` to `accepted`.
