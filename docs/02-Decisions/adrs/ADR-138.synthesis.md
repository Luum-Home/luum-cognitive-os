## Decision
Commit `manifests/flow-contract-schema.yaml`, a required shape every flow primitive registered after this ADR must satisfy (partial conformance rejected at registration). Key required fields: `flow_id` (unique kebab-case), `lifecycle_state` (must start at `lab`), `input_source` with a `determinism` flag (deterministic required for promotion beyond `advisory`), `success_condition.verifier` (must run from CI without maintainer-machine assumptions), `sandboxed_write_paths` (excludes `manifests/`, `rules/`, ADRs, and `.cognitive-os/runtime/` by default), `blocked_actions` (hardcoded minimum five: auto_merge, auto_promote_core_or_team, invent_evidence, bypass_governance_gate, direct_main_push — removals not allowed), `human_approval_required: true` (hardcoded, a flow proposing to remove it fails validation), `evidence_shape.independence` (mirrors the anti-self-validation flags from `external-adoption-evidence.yaml`), a `framing_exercise_statement` with five required axes per ADR-137, at least one `non_goals` entry, and at least one `falsifiable_when` condition. Critically, the schema is committed as **exemplary**, not **shared**, status — it only generalizes to a cross-flow contract after a *second* flow registers against it unmodified; if the second flow needs extensions, they land as ADR-138a rather than silently mutating this schema.

## Why
Explicitly designed to prevent two failure modes: premature generalization (a schema derived from one flow encoding that flow's idiosyncrasies as universal truths) and drift (a schema that grows fields per-flow without a promotion rule becomes a checklist, not a contract). The schema is intentionally minimal — enough to make the first flow (`vuln-remediation-flow`) safe, not enough to anticipate every future flow.

## Consequences
Positive: every flow inherits ADR-134/135's propose-only safety properties (blocked actions, human approval) without re-declaring them; the framing-exercise statement requirement gets a concrete, registration-time-checked home instead of remaining architecture-doc prose; a new contributor can read a flow's contract YAML alone to learn what it promises, refuses, and when it's considered broken. Negative: every field is a maintenance commitment, braked only by the promotion rule; early flows may feel boilerplate-heavy (an intended cost — propose-only was previously implicit and survived only via maintainer attention); the schema may anchor design too tightly to the first flow, mitigated by the ADR-138a extension path.

## Status & current state
Accepted, implemented (`manifests/flow-contract-schema.yaml` exists). The schema remains at **exemplary** status (not yet promoted to **shared**) until a second flow registers without modification — that promotion event and its enforcement script `scripts/cos-flow-contract-audit` had not yet occurred/been created as of this ADR. The first flow, `skills/vuln-remediation-flow/flow_contract.yaml`, registers against this schema. `scripts/cos-flow-register.sh` (the CI registration-audit implementation) is noted as "to be created" — until it exists, conformance is checked by the maintainer reading the YAML manually.
