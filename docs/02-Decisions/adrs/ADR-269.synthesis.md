## Decision
A four-primitive enforcement layer operating at three time points (pre-execute gate, post-rewrite ledger entry, periodic audit). Primitive 1: `manifests/history-rewrite-ledger.yaml`, an append-only registry of every history rewrite (timestamp, operator, `adr_ref`, reason, bundle path, SHA before/after), enforced append-only by a contract test asserting prior entries stay byte-identical. Primitive 2: `hooks/history-rewrite-documented.sh` (SessionStart), which cross-references every `.cognitive-os/recovery/pre-history-sanitization-*.bundle` against the ledger and emits a visible, non-blocking WARNING for any orphan bundle. Primitive 3: `scripts/cos-history-rewrite-audit` CLI (`--list`, `--orphans`, `--register <bundle> --adr ADR-NNN --reason "..."`) for inspection and remediation. Primitive 4: both `cos-history-sanitization --execute` and `cos-filter-repo-wrap.sh` now REQUIRE an `--adr-ref ADR-NNN` flag pointing at an Accepted ADR, failing at invocation without it, and auto-write a ledger entry on success; direct `git filter-repo` calls are added to `hooks/destructive-git-blocker.sh`'s DESTRUCTIVE_PATTERN, forcing all rewrite paths through the wrapper or an explicit, audit-logged `COS_ALLOW_DESTRUCTIVE_GIT=1` bypass.

## Why
Advisory documentation ("operator should document") does not scale to multi-agent workflows where any agent can run destructive commands — it is exactly what nearly failed on 2026-05-11. A structured, append-only, machine-readable ledger (rather than relying on ADRs alone, which are narrative and assume contemporaneous authorship) enables seconds-fast `--list`/`--orphans` audits and cross-validates that a referenced ADR actually exists and is Accepted, not merely Proposed or Superseded. The startup banner (rather than a commit-time gate) was chosen because bundles are evidence of *past* rewrites, not active mutations — blocking commits over historical undocumented rewrites would be punitive to forward work.

## Consequences
Positive: mechanical defense against a "concealment narrative" in future IP discovery or compliance review — every rewrite carries a contemporaneous ADR or an explicit, logged bypass; orphan bundles become visible instead of silently accumulating; existing wrapper safety machinery (ADR-242 backup/recovery) stays intact, this ADR only layers documentation on top. Negative: new friction for legitimate quick sanitizations (must author an ADR first — mitigated by a provided template); the startup banner risks becoming ignored noise if orphans persist (mitigated by proposing inclusion in a daily metrics dashboard); adding filter-repo to DESTRUCTIVE_PATTERN may surprise operators doing routine repo maintenance. Unmitigated risks acknowledged explicitly: an operator with ledger write access could forge entries (defended only by the append-only contract test + commit signing); an operator could delete recovery bundles to remove evidence (explicitly out of scope, deferred to a future backup-retention ADR); the ADR itself could be modified after the fact (defended by ADR status/supersession chain and the ADR file's own git history).

## Status & current state
Accepted, implemented. Implementation files present: `manifests/history-rewrite-ledger.yaml`, `hooks/history-rewrite-documented.sh`, `scripts/cos-history-rewrite-audit`, `lib/history_sanitization.py`, `lib/history_rewrite_ledger.py`. Explicitly consolidates/extends the existing history-rewrite safety chain (ADR-218, ADR-242, ADR-243, ADR-246) rather than scope-creeping into a new domain (documented via `relationship_chain_exempt: true` in frontmatter). Three open questions remain UNSURE at time of writing: whether the ledger should live in `manifests/` (committed, chosen) vs. an operator-private location; retention policy for recovery bundles (currently indefinite, pending a future ADR); and whether the `--adr-ref` requirement composes correctly with an active ADR-246 release-transaction-freeze during an emergency rewrite (hypothesized yes, untested).
