## Decision
Establish a contract requiring a §Operational Guide section in maintainer-tier ADRs that introduce a new capability (new script/manifest/hook/schema/public surface), excluding tombstones, superseded, and pure-architectural ADRs. The guide must include at least 3 of 5 sub-sections: what changes for the operator, what this answers/doesn't, daily operational pattern, when sources disagree, and reading guide for cold readers. Phase 1 (this ADR) ships an audit (`scripts/cos-operational-guide-audit.py`) that reports presence/coverage, and a gate (`hooks/adr-section-validator.sh`, check `require_operational_guide`) that WARNs by default (exit 0) and blocks under `COS_STRICT_ADR_VALIDATION=1` (exit 2) — enforcement applies only to ADRs edited after this ADR, with existing ADRs handled via a prioritized backfill list (P0: accepted ≤30 days, P1: maintainer-tier with implementation_files, P2: older maintainer-tier). An exemption escape hatch (`<!-- adr-274-exempt: <reason> -->`) lets authors opt out with an audited reason.

## Why
The 2026-05-12 adversarial review of ADR-273 found operational context (how to use a capability, what it changes for the operator) consistently missing from ADR bodies even when the architectural sections were complete — and the one-off fix for ADR-273 was a single-instance patch with no generalizing convention, no validating gate, and no audit of which other ADRs had the same gap. Root cause shared with ADR-273: COS produces capabilities faster than documentation discipline keeps up, so operational context perpetually trails artifacts unless a gate forces it.

## Consequences
Positive: future maintainer-tier capability ADRs cannot ship without operational context (advisory first, strict on demand); the backfill scope becomes explicit and visible via audit rather than invisible; §Operational Guide presence feeds into Trust Report scoring per the ADR-105 bilateral-evidence principle (missing guide = LOW trust signal even with all other sections present). Negative: backfilling existing P1/P2 ADRs is real work (~15 min per ADR), and the audit only makes the scope visible — sweeping through it remains an explicit operator/sprint decision.
