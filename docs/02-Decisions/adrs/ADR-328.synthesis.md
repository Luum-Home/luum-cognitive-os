## Decision
Add a first-class friction-vs-catch ratio to the governance ROI dashboard, exposed via `cos status` / `cos governance roi`. Three executable surfaces: (1) read-side ROI/ratio/catch-ledger/phase-policy exposure; (2) write-side optional catch-review prompts on blocked hooks (default `skip`, never forced) plus `cos governance catch log` to record reviewed verdicts (`confirmed_valid_block`, `false_positive_override`, `silent_loss_prevented`, `high_blast_radius_catch`) into an append-only `.cognitive-os/metrics/governance-catches.jsonl` ledger; (3) an enforcement adapter, `cos governance policy --category <category>`, that hooks query to check whether their category may hard-block in the current project phase. The ratio (weighted blocking events / weighted confirmed useful catches, with severity weights low=0.5 to critical=3.0) is bucketed `paying` (<=2x), `watch` (2-5x), or `cut` (>5x); with zero reviewed catches the status is honestly `unknown` rather than assumed-correct. Phase-aware policy tables (reconstruction/stabilization/production/maintenance) define which guard categories may hard-block per phase; unknown phase/category defaults to advisory, never blocking, to prevent new low-signal guards from becoming always-on friction by accident.

## Why
The core insight: governance only pays for itself under high blast radius, multi-agent contention, security sensitivity, release risk, destructive operations, or real risk of silent work loss — for low-risk exploratory single-developer work the same guards can cost more in friction than they prevent in harm. Without a reviewed catch ledger, a dashboard showing "N blocks" cannot tell whether those blocks were justified or just noise, so the ADR requires operator-reviewed evidence before the ratio can claim a guard is worth keeping.

## Consequences
Positive: the OS can measure whether governance is paying for real catches rather than assuming more blocking is inherently safer; false positives become visible and reviewable; phase-specific policy stops always-on friction from dominating exploratory work; a concrete recut signal exists (>5x means cut or demote). Negative: the ratio is only as good as operator review discipline — without logged catches, status stays `unknown` by design; the benefit model is heuristic, not financial accounting; phase policy is coarse and individual guards still need local judgment; full per-guard adoption requires touching every hard-blocking hook one at a time.

## Status & current state
Accepted, explicitly PARTIAL: read-side dashboard, write-side catch logging, optional blocked-hook prompts, weighted severity normalization, and the executable phase-policy adapter are implemented and wired into the initial hard-blocking hooks (`destructive-git-blocker`, `destructive-rm-blocker`, `direct-main-guard`) and the next high-friction set (`protected-config-write-guard`, `network-egress-guard`, `release-guard`). Full per-guard phase-policy enforcement remains incremental — every additional hard-blocking hook must call the policy adapter before returning a block.
